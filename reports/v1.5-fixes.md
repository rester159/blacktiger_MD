# v1.5: visible records, high-score table, shop prices and dialogue

The title artwork's captured score cells are cleared during conversion. The native title renderer draws the current SRAM-backed high score over that area and redraws it when the value changes. The Arcade/Home selector now includes HIGH SCORES. Qualifying completed sessions enter three-letter initials, with Up/Down letter selection, A advancing/confirming, and B moving back. The table stores five ranked scores and their Arcade/Home/Boss Rush modes. Debug sessions do not enter the table. A continued session records its highest credit score once the session ends.

The v1.4 single-score records remain at logical SRAM offsets 0 and 16. The table adds two 64-byte journal slots at 32 and 96 with generation numbers and checksums. Each destination is invalidated before writing and committed last, leaving the previous slot recoverable. The cartridge advertises odd-address SRAM through $20013F. Existing v1.4 high scores seed a new table. The emulator must load the old battery save under the new ROM filename; renaming a ROM does not itself rename the emulator's save file. No save files were found in this workspace to rename automatically.

The US catalog uses the existing original price schedule 0: weapons 100, 1000, 2400, 9600; armor 80, 300, 800, 1600; key 30; antidote 150. Combat difficulty retains its existing setting and source damage table. Original alternative price schedules remain in the extracted data and their oracle tests; public play uses the requested US catalog. Price fields are fully padded to the next column so old digits cannot remain, and the adjacent EXIT label is preserved.

The renderer lowers only sprite entries intersecting the dialogue band (screen rows 48–79) while in RESCUE mode. High-priority opaque dialogue pixels therefore win over the ladder enemy; other sprites retain their original priority. This adjustment runs once after sprite assembly rather than adding checks to every normal gameplay sprite.

Regression coverage includes literal shop labels and corresponding purchase deductions, long-to-short price replacement, preserved EXIT lettering, the actual ladder-sentry constructor crossing the dialogue band, and all 6,568 opaque message pixels with and without the enemy. Score tests cover v1.4 migration, displayed title digits, ranked insertion and eviction, letter wrapping/backtracking, nonqualifying runs, power cycles, all 52 single-byte table corruptions, and the existing single-score interruption tests. The Home/main version stamp is v1.5.

The restored SEGA/CAPCOM startup remains in a separate ignored local build, not in the source-only repository. Release ROMs and source assets remain excluded from Git.

Final validation: all 175 test scripts passed on the source-only cartridge (`3897f030f5671a82b23f0dae3c7d82909fbf3eb6eceeddb5eadfd5ebb75a11b7`), including both existing busy-scene thresholds. The local cartridge with the opening sequence (`df60aab00e2cd650c77ad067f65bed318aa4ac6b6c5b1ed5b7ff59b01bc13543`) passed 174/175 scripts; its level-3 worst one-second presentation window remains 24 FPS against the 25 FPS floor. The all-level and detailed performance profiles and 48 sprite pixel fixtures passed for both cartridges. Both are 4 MiB and passed checksum and strict packaging checks. Generated ROMs and all boot assets stay local.
